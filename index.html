1. Common Human Infectious Diseases: Pathogens, Organs & Diagnostics

Disease Causative Pathogen Target Organ / Site Mode of Transmission Diagnostic Symptoms • Confirmatory Test
Typhoid Salmonella typhi (Bacterium) Small intestine → blood → other organs Contaminated food and drinking water High fever (39°–40°C), stomach pain, constipation, intestinal perforation; confirmed by Widal test (Mary Mallon / Typhoid Mary)
Pneumonia Streptococcus pneumoniae & Haemophilus influenzae Alveoli (air-filled sacs) of lungs Inhaling droplets/aerosols, sharing utensils Alveoli filled with fluid, chills, cough; severe cases show cyanosis (lips & fingernails turn gray to bluish)
Common Cold Rhino viruses Nose & upper respiratory passage (NOT lungs) Inhalation of droplets, pens, books, cups, doorknobs Nasal congestion, sore throat, hoarseness, cough, headache; lasts 3–7 days
Malaria Plasmodium vivax, P. malariae, P. falciparum Liver cells → Red Blood Cells (RBCs) Bite of female Anopheles mosquito RBC rupture releases toxic haemozoin → recurring chills & fever every 3–4 days. P. falciparum causes fatal malignant malaria
Amoebiasis Entamoeba histolytica (Protozoan) Large intestine Houseflies as mechanical carriers from faeces to food/water Constipation, abdominal pain and cramps, stools with excess mucous and dark blood clots
Ascariasis Ascaris lumbricoides (Roundworm) Intestinal tract Contaminated water, vegetables, soil containing eggs Internal bleeding, muscular pain, fever, anemia, blockage of the intestinal passage
Filariasis (Elephantiasis) Wuchereria bancrofti & W. malayi (Filarial worm) Lymphatic vessels of lower limbs and genital organs Bite of female mosquito vector (Culex) Slowly developing chronic inflammation, gross elephantine swelling of legs and deformities of scrotum/genitals
Ringworm Microsporum, Trichophyton, Epidermophyton (Fungi) Keratinized skin, nails, scalp, groin folds Soil, towels, clothes, combs of infected persons Appearance of dry, scaly ring-like lesions with intense itching; thrive in warm, moist skin folds

2. Complete Life Cycle of Plasmodium (Two Hosts: Human & Mosquito)

Human Host (Asexual Phase • Intermediate Host)

  1. Inoculation: Female Anopheles bites human, injecting infectious motile sporozoites stored in its salivary glands into blood.
  2. Pre-erythrocytic Cycle: Sporozoites reach the liver within 30 minutes, multiply asexually (schizogony) in hepatocytes, bursting liver cells to release cryptozoites/merozoites into blood.
  3. Erythrocytic Cycle: Parasites penetrate red blood cells (RBCs), feed on hemoglobin, reproduce asexually, and burst RBCs.
  4. Fever Paroxysm: Rupture of RBCs releases haemozoin toxin into plasma → chills and high fever recurring every 3–4 days.
  5. Gametocytogenesis: Some merozoites differentiate into sexual stages — male (micro) and female (macro) gametocytes in human RBCs.

Mosquito Host (Sexual Phase • Definitive Host & Vector)

  1. Blood Meal: Female Anopheles mosquito bites an infected human and takes up mature gametocytes with RBCs.
  2. Fertilization: Fertilization between micro- and macrogametes takes place within the lumen of the mosquito's gut.
  3. Ookinete & Oocyst: The motile zygote (ookinete) penetrates the stomach wall, forming an encysted oocyst on the outer gut surface.
  4. Sporogony: Oocyst undergoes asexual nuclear divisions to form thousands of slender sporozoites.
  5. Migration: Mature infective sporozoites burst from oocyst and migrate through hemolymph to lodge in the salivary glands of the mosquito, ready to infect a new human host.

3. Immunity, Antibody Structure ($H_2L_2$) & Graft Rejection

4 Innate Immunity Barriers

  • • Physical: Skin (keratinized stratum corneum) and mucus coating of respiratory, gastrointestinal, and urogenital tracts.
  • • Physiological: Gastric HCl in stomach, lysozyme in saliva, tears from eyes.
  • • Cellular: Polymorphonuclear leukocytes (PMNL-neutrophils), blood monocytes, tissue macrophages, and Natural Killer (NK) lymphocytes.
  • • Cytokine: Interferons secreted by virus-infected cells to protect adjacent healthy cells.

Acquired Immunity Features

  • • Pathogen-specific & Memory-based: Primary response is of low intensity; secondary (anamnestic) response is rapid and highly intensified.
  • • Humoral Immune Response: B-lymphocytes produce an army of proteins called antibodies in blood plasma.
  • • Cell-Mediated Immunity (CMI): Mediated by T-lymphocytes. Differentiates self from non-self and is directly responsible for graft rejection in organ transplants.

Antibody Molecule ($H_2L_2$)

  • • Structure: 4 polypeptide chains — 2 identical small light (L) chains and 2 identical longer heavy (H) chains joined by disulfide bridges.
  • • Antigen-Binding Site: Formed by variable regions ($V_H$ and $V_L$) at the N-terminal Fab ends.
  • • Major Classes:
    • IgA: In colostrum and secretions.
    • IgE: Mediates allergic reactions.
    • IgG: Most abundant, crosses placenta.
    • IgM: Pentameric, first response.
Organ Transplantation • Tissue Matching: Grafts cannot be taken from just anyone because the host's cell-mediated immunity (CMI) will reject foreign MHC antigens. Tissue matching and blood group matching are mandatory before transplant, and recipients must take immunosuppressants (e.g. Cyclosporin A) for life.

4. Lymphoid Organs, AIDS, Cancer & Drug Abuse Summary

Lymphoid Organs Overview

• Primary Lymphoid Organs: Bone marrow (produces all blood cells) and Thymus (lobate organ near heart; large at birth, atrophies by puberty). Sites of lymphocyte origin and maturation.

• Secondary Lymphoid Organs: Spleen (bean-shaped, blood filter, erythrocyte reservoir), Lymph nodes (trap tissue antigens), tonsils, Peyer's patches, appendix.

• MALT: Mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue lines major respiratory, digestive, and urogenital tracts; constitutes 50% of human lymphoid tissue.

AIDS (HIV Retrovirus)

• Structure: Enveloped virus containing diploid single-stranded RNA and reverse transcriptase.

• Replication: Enters macrophages → viral DNA synthesized by reverse transcriptase → integrates into host DNA → macrophage acts as an 'HIV factory'.

• Immunodeficiency: HIV simultaneously attacks and lyses helper T-lymphocytes ($T_H$), causing progressive $T_H$ collapse → opportunistic infections (Mycobacterium, Toxoplasma, fungi). Diagnosed by ELISA.

Cancer Oncology Hallmarks

• Loss of Contact Inhibition: Cancer cells proliferate continuously, forming benign or malignant tumors.

• Metastasis: Malignant cells slough off into blood, lodging at distant sites to form secondary tumors (most feared property).

• Carcinogens: Physical (X-rays, gamma, UV rays), Chemical (tobacco smoke), Biological (oncogenic viruses with viral oncogenes; cellular proto-oncogenes c-onc).

• Treatment: Surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and biological response modifiers like α-interferon.

Drugs and Chemical Substances Abused

• Opioids (Heroin / Smack): Diacetylmorphine from latex of Papaver somniferum. Depressant, slows down body functions.

• Cannabinoids: From Cannabis sativa inflorescences (marijuana, hashish, charas, ganja). Inhaled/ingested; affects cardiovascular system.

• Cocaine / Crack: From Erythroxylum coca. Blocks dopamine reuptake; CNS stimulant causing euphoria; excessive doses cause hallucinations.

• Hallucinogens: Atropa belladonna and Datura.

• Tobacco: Nicotine alkaloid stimulates adrenal glands to release adrenaline/nor-adrenaline → elevated BP and heart rate; CO causes hypoxia.
